04 / Lore

The story begins here.

The First Fall

No one remembers the exact night the first star fell. The oldest records only mention a light over the northern sky, bright enough to turn midnight white for a few seconds. By morning, something had changed. Small fragments of light were scattered across the land, buried in fields, lodged between cliffs, and resting in places no one had seen before. They looked like pieces of stars, but they were warm to the touch, almost alive, and every fragment carried a different shape, glow and weight.

At first, people treated them like miracles. Villages formed around the larger fragments, collectors began searching farther from home, and strange machines were built to pull more of them from the sky. The more stars that were found, the stranger the world became. New paths appeared where there had been nothing before. Buildings began showing up on maps with no record of who built them. Some collectors claimed their stars moved while nobody was watching, always turning toward the same part of the sky, as if waiting for something else to arrive.

Years passed and the falling never stopped. Most people eventually stopped asking where the stars came from and learned to live with them. The ones who kept asking left behind records that were later hidden, burned or simply went missing. One surviving page contains a set of coordinates that do not match any known place, followed by a sentence written hard enough to tear through the paper: the first fall was not the arrival. Everything after that page is gone. The only thing written on the back is a date that has not happened yet.

Stop calling them stars.

Stars do not turn when you look away. They do not gather over the same house three nights in a row. And they do not know your name. If one lands near you, leave it where it fell.
